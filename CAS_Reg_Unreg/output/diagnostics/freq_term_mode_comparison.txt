FOR THE MEMO (Section 5.6)
----------------------------------------------------------------
The regulated flow at a given AEP carries uncertainty from two
independent sources: how well the unregulated frequency curve is
known (HEC-SSP confidence limits), and how much storm shape affects
attenuation (scatter of the regulated points about the fitted
transform). Per EM 1110-2-1619 (29 Sep 2025) Sec. 4-4.b(3), Eq. 4-6,
independent standard deviations combine by root-sum-of-squares, with
the upper and lower side of each source kept separate (Sec. 4-6a) so
the real asymmetry in both sources survives the combination.

Before combining, the frequency term is converted into regulated-cfs
units by evaluating the fitted unregulated-to-regulated transform at
the unregulated confidence bound -- the same curve used for the best
estimate -- rather than treated as an unregulated-cfs quantity added
directly to the regulated-cfs transform term. This keeps both terms
in the same units before they are combined:

  freq_term_hi = Transform(Unreg_97.5) - Transform(Unreg_best)
  freq_term_lo = Transform(Unreg_best) - Transform(Unreg_2.5)
  transform_term_hi/lo = the fitted curve's own confidence spread,
                         evaluated at Unreg_best

  Upper = RegBest + sqrt(freq_term_hi^2 + transform_term_hi^2)
  Lower = RegBest - sqrt(freq_term_lo^2 + transform_term_lo^2)

at the 90% two-sided level (z = 1.645).

WHY NOT THE REVIEWER'S FORMULA READ LITERALLY
----------------------------------------------------------------
Read literally, the reviewer's formula subtracts unregulated flows
directly (Unreg_97.5 - Unreg_best) and adds that raw cfs value to the
regulated-cfs transform term -- implicitly treating a 1-cfs change in
unregulated flow as a 1-cfs change in regulated flow. That assumption
is false through most of this curve, which reduces peak flow 30-37
percent. It happens to land within a few percent of the curve-based
result over much of this particular transform's range (Appendix:
comparison table below), but that agreement is a property of this
transform's shape, not something a different basin or a re-fit
transform would be guaranteed to reproduce. Evaluating the actual
fitted curve removes the assumption instead of relying on it holding
by coincidence.
